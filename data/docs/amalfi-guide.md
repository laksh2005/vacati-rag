# Amalfi Coast Destination Guide

## When to go

May and late September are the best value months: sea temperatures are swimmable, restaurants are open, and rates sit roughly 30% below peak. July and August are hot and crowded, and the coast road can take twice as long to drive. November to March many coastal properties and restaurants close entirely.

## Getting there

Naples International Airport (NAP) is the closest airport, about 90 minutes by road to Positano in normal traffic. A private transfer costs roughly EUR 130 one way. The cheaper route is the Curreri bus to Sorrento, then the SITA coastal bus.

From June to September, ferries run from Naples, Sorrento, and Salerno to Positano and Amalfi. Ferries are usually faster and more pleasant than the coast road in high season, but they are cancelled in rough seas.

## Getting around

Driving the SS163 coast road is scenic but slow, and parking in Positano is scarce and expensive at roughly EUR 6 per hour. Most guests do better with the SITA bus, the seasonal ferries, and scooters or taxis for short hops.

## What to do

Walk the Path of the Gods from Bomerano to Nocelle, a 6 km ridge trail taking about three hours with significant descent at the end. Day trip to Capri by ferry. Visit the Amalfi cathedral and the paper museum. Book a lemon grove tour in Minori.

## Local practicalities

Most restaurants serve dinner from 19:30. Many small shops close between 13:30 and 16:30. Cash is still useful in the smaller villages, though cards are accepted almost everywhere on the main strip.
